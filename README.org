* The problem

Mechanical-keyboard sound is the hobby's most-discussed, least-measurable
property. Reviewers say /thocky/, /poppy/, /creamy/, /marble/. Manufacturer
demos are recorded in soundproof booths with $3,000 microphones. You watch
a YouTube clip, buy the switch, install it on /your/ board on /your/ desk,
and it sounds nothing like the clip.

There is no shared, comparable, reproducible language for switch sound.

* What thock is

A measurement instrument. You record /your/ switches under /your/
conditions, and you get back something quantitative:

- the actual frequencies present in the press (the housing's resonant
  signature)
- the full envelope from attack to housing ring-out
- both the press and the release wavefronts of each key cycle
- a /fingerprint/ — the averaged spectrum across every press of a given
  switch, the thing that is genuinely "the sound of this switch"

You can compare two switches side by side. You can let a virtual typist
play either one back at any WPM, using /your/ samples, with realistic
English digraph timing — so you hear what 60 seconds of actual
paragraph-typing on that switch would sound like. Not a single press in
isolation; the whole texture.

Single-page web app. Static. Everything runs in your browser. Recordings
land on your disk via the File System Access API. Nothing is uploaded.

Live: *https://thock.mwlabs.be*

* Who it's for

** The curious
"What does this switch I just bought actually sound like? Is it as thocky
as people said?"

Record 50 presses, look at the fingerprint, hit /typist/, hear it as
typing.

** The comparison shopper
"Holy Panda or Boba U4T?"

Record both. Their fingerprints sit next to each other. The typist plays
either one with one click — same text, same WPM, same recording
conditions. The difference is audible and visible.

** The reviewer / seller
"I want to publish a switch-sound library that's directly comparable to
other people's libraries — because we used the same instrument."

Record a full dataset (50-200 samples per switch), bundle it as a preset,
publish. Anyone running thock can import and compare against their own
captures. Reviews stop being subjective adjectives and start being
overlaid spectra.

** The hobbyist explorer
"I want to browse switches I'll never own and hear what typing on them
would sound like."

Open the preset library, pick a switch, hit /typist/. Type something.
Listen. Move on.

* The four panes

** capture
Enable the mic, choose a folder, click /arm/. Every press above the
auto-learned noise floor is segmented and written as a WAV in real time.
No metronome, no review step — just press as you would type, and each
keystroke lands on disk. Vary your force and speed; the fingerprint
averages over the lot.

** switch
Your library, one card per switch, color-coded. The fingerprint canvas
shows the averaged smoothed spectrum with the top resonant peaks labelled
(/428 Hz/, /1.24 kHz/, /4.5 kHz/ …). The samples grid shows every press,
sortable by amplitude or recording order.

** inspect
Click any sample to see its waveform (onset-aligned, with =DOWN= / =UP=
markers for clicky switches), its per-sample FFT, and a magma-colormap
STFT spectrogram.

** typist
Pick a switch, set a WPM, pick a text mode (top-100 English words /
quotes / pangrams / random). Hit start. The typist schedules samples
ahead of time using AudioContext, plays press + release wavefronts with
realistic dwell, cycles through your samples so no two strokes are
identical, and renders the letters above the keystroke waveforms in
sync.

* The recording philosophy

A "press of switch X" is a /concept/ in Rand's sense: a class of acoustic
events that share an essential signature (the conceptual common
denominator — frequency-domain identity of the housing, attack character,
release character) and differ only in measurements that don't matter
(velocity, finger angle, mic distance jitter).

Thock's capture pipeline is built to that idea:

- /Preserve the CCD./ The full press — attack, click(s), housing ring,
  release wavefront — is captured. The tail-close threshold is below the
  trigger threshold so housing ring-out is never chopped.
- /Exclude non-units./ Spectral and temporal sanity checks before a
  recording is written. Door slams, voice, chair creaks don't share the
  CCD of a keyswitch press — they don't enter the concept.
- /Measurements omitted ≠ measurements nonexistent./ Each WAV gets a
  sidecar JSON: peak, RMS, attack ms, sub-event count and spacing,
  spectral centroid, captured noise-floor. The fingerprint can re-verify
  that every contributing sample really is a unit of the concept (and
  reject outliers).
- /Commensurable./ Float-32 WAV format, no quantization on the quiet
  tail. Session-calibrated noise floor instead of a drifting EMA.
- /Differentiated./ The trigger uses a high-passed envelope so room
  rumble can't inflate the threshold; the threshold tracks the noise
  /in the band where keyswitch transients live/.

* Running locally

Requires [[https://devenv.sh][devenv]]:

#+BEGIN_SRC sh
devenv up        # static server on http://127.0.0.1:8765
#+END_SRC

Or any static server:

#+BEGIN_SRC sh
python -m http.server 8765
#+END_SRC

Click /choose folder/ to pick where samples go. Firefox/Safari fall back
to OPFS (browser-managed filesystem, functionally identical, just not
visible to other tools).

* Adding presets

After recording your dataset:

#+BEGIN_SRC sh
python scripts/bundle_presets.py /path/to/your/samples --clear
#+END_SRC

Copies each =<switch>/*.wav= into =presets/<switch>/= and regenerates
=presets/index.json=. Optional =meta.json= per switch:

#+BEGIN_SRC json
{
  "name": "Choc v2 Red",
  "description": "Kailh Choc v2 Red — 50 gf linear",
  "color": "#ff6b6b"
}
#+END_SRC

Commit and push. The site picks them up.

* Stack

- Vanilla HTML / CSS / JavaScript — no framework, no build
- Web Audio API + AudioWorklet for capture (sub-batch latency)
- File System Access API + IndexedDB handle (real folder) or OPFS
  (browser-managed) — backend-agnostic above the FS layer
- Fonts: Major Mono Display (wordmark), Geist Mono (everything else)

* Monetization

Thock's core loop — record, fingerprint, inspect, share — stays free.
There is no good reason to gate someone from analyzing the sound of
their own keyboard.

What scales:

** typist tokens (the primary lever)
The typist is the part of the app you /spend time in/. Listening to a
preset switch type out a paragraph is the moment people get hooked and
the moment they're comparing options. It also accidentally makes a good
metering surface: minutes of audible playback, not keystrokes captured.

- Free: 60 seconds of typist per session, generous enough to evaluate a
  switch. Capture and fingerprint are unmetered.
- Tokens: $5 = 60 minutes of typist time. $20 = 5 hours. $50 = lifetime.
- Tokens cover hosting and fund the curated preset library + the eventual
  server-side ML synthesis tier.

The honest pitch: capture is free because it costs nothing to run; the
typist is metered because (a) it's the leisure feature you'd happily pay
for and (b) future versions will do real-time server-side synthesis from
your samples, which has a real cost.

** preset marketplace (the secondary lever)
Verified preset packs from manufacturers, reviewers, and switch designers.
$2-10 per pack. Revenue-shared 70/30 with the contributor.

** pro tier (optional)
$5/mo: unlimited typist, cloud sync of your library across devices,
shareable A/B comparison URLs with custom branding, no marketplace fees
on packs you publish.

** what stays free, forever
- Recording your own switches
- Inspecting fingerprints and samples
- Importing the un-curated community preset list
- Exporting your samples
- Running the app on your own server (it's static)
